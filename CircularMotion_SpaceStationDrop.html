You let go. Nothing pulls the ball. So why does it hit the floor?

Because the ball was already moving sideways with you when you released it. It keeps going in a straight line, and the curved floor swings around to meet it. Watch the same drop from outside the station and from aboard.

Outside view (not spinning)

No forces after release. The ball travels in a straight line at the speed your hand had.

Your view (spinning with the station)

The floor seems still. The ball seems to fall, and drifts backward as it does.

From outside: no fall at all

Your hand is carried around a circle, so at the moment you open it the ball has a sideways velocity of spin rate × radius. With no force on it, it coasts in a straight line. A straight line drawn inside a circle always reaches the rim, so the floor and the ball meet. Nothing pulled it down; the station's geometry did the work.

From aboard: it looks like gravity

You are rotating, so you describe motion in a turning frame. In that frame the ball appears to accelerate outward (centrifugal effect) and to veer against the spin (Coriolis effect). These "forces" are bookkeeping for the frame you are in, not pushes from anything. Raise the drop height or slow the spin to make the backward drift obvious.

Why it misses your feet

Your hand was closer to the hub than your feet, so it moved slower. While the ball coasts, your feet race ahead along the rim. On a real 100 m station with a 2 m drop the miss is a few centimetres; from high up near the hub it becomes huge.
